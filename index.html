Reading tips
Shortcuts that make signs and names click faster
Every letter is said

No silent letters and no spelling surprises. Say each letter in turn and you've read the word.

‑ИЯ at the end is ‑ia

The Latin spelling writes it ‑ia, and it sounds like "ee‑ya".

‑ЦИЯ is English ‑tion

Lots of borrowed words end this way. If you can read ‑ЦИЯ, you can guess the English word.

Ъ is "uh" but written a

It's the most common letter you won't recognise. In Latin spellings it becomes a.

Щ is "sht"

In Russian it's "shch", but in Bulgarian it's always "sht".

Address shorthand

Street signs and maps abbreviate these, usually before the name.

ул.streetбул.boulevardпл.squareСв.Saint
Surnames come in pairs

Men's names end ‑ов or ‑ев, and women's add an ‑а.

ИвановIvanovИвановаIvanovaПетроваPetrova
Handwriting and fancy fonts look different

On menus, shopfronts and in italics, lower case can change shape. д can look like g, т like m, and и like u. Capitals stay the same, so read those first.

Check yourself with the Latin

Metro and main street signs in Sofia often show the Latin spelling too. Read the Cyrillic first, then look down to check.

Yes and no

Traditionally a nod means no and a shake means yes. Many people in Sofia switch for visitors, so go by the words: да is yes and не is no.
